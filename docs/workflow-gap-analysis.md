# Workflow Gap Analysis

## Purpose

This document evaluates the current-state recruitment and medical-record retrieval workflow to identify operational gaps, data-visibility limitations, duplicate work, and opportunities for process improvement.

## Gap 1 — Fragmented Workflow Tracking

Patient information and workflow status are maintained across multiple operational systems, including a shared recruitment tracker, a separate medical-record retrieval tracker, vendor portals, and a CRM.

The shared recruitment tracker and medical-record retrieval tracker contain overlapping patient information and require manual updates as patients progress through the workflow.

This creates fragmented visibility across operational stages and introduces opportunities for duplicate data entry, inconsistent status information, and delayed updates between systems.

## Gap 1 — Fragmented Workflow Tracking

Patient progression is tracked across separate operational tools and spreadsheets rather than through a unified workflow view.

Different stages of the process require separate tracking for recruitment, ROI authorization, and medical-record retrieval. As patients transition between stages, information must be manually maintained across the relevant trackers.

This structure limits end-to-end visibility into the patient journey and creates opportunities for duplicate data entry, inconsistent status information, and delayed updates.

## Gap 2 — Operational Knowledge Is Maintained Outside the Core Workflow

Additional spreadsheets are required to maintain information needed for day-to-day medical-record retrieval operations.

A facility reference tracker stores reusable contact information so that staff do not need to repeatedly research the same facility when submitting new requests.

A separate medical-record retrieval tracker captures request activity and supports follow-up decisions based on observed facility turnaround behavior.

Because this operational knowledge exists outside the primary workflow system, facility information and historical performance are not directly connected to the patient and request data they support.

## Gap 3 — Email-Dependent Workflow Handoffs

Patient handoffs between operational stages rely on email communication.

After a patient passes the initial eligibility screening, the intake specialist sends an email to initiate the ROI authorization stage. Because the handoff depends on a separate communication channel rather than a shared workflow status or task queue, patient transitions can be missed or delayed.

This creates a risk that an eligible patient remains inactive even though the preceding workflow stage has been completed.

## Gap 4 — Spreadsheet-Dependent Follow-Up Logic

ROI follow-up management relies on spreadsheet-based logic to identify patients requiring action.

Data validation, conditional formulas, and visual indicators are used to track follow-up status and surface patients approaching required intervention points.

While this provides operational support, the follow-up logic is embedded within an individual spreadsheet rather than integrated into the broader recruitment workflow. As a result, task visibility and follow-up management depend on the maintenance and use of the tracker itself.

## Gap 5 — Lack of Standardized Operational Procedures

The workflow does not have a standardized operational process for managing work across each stage.

Staff members may develop individual methods, trackers, and organizational practices to manage assigned responsibilities as long as required outcomes are completed.

This creates dependence on individual working methods and limits consistency across the operation. Effective practices developed by one team member may remain isolated rather than becoming shared operational knowledge.

The absence of standardized procedures also makes the workflow more difficult to transfer, audit, scale, and improve systematically.
